Week 1 Practical: The Mock Patient

Everything from the first five days, brought together into one complete chairside walkthrough.

How today works

The morning is the master check-off, completed with a senior clinical trainer. The afternoon is the mock patient scenario. Neither is a test you can fail quietly. Anything not yet solid gets noted and retrained, which is the entire point.

Part 2The mock patient

Scenario assignment

  • Patient: Emma, simulated training patient
  • Appointment: braces adjustment, 30 minutes
  • Chair: Chair 6
  1. Chart verificationOpen Dolphin, locate Emma, confirm the photo, verify the scheduled adjustment, and read the previous notes.
  2. Operatory checkVerify Chair 6 is sanitized, prepped with an adjustment tray, and that safety glasses are ready.
  3. Reception pickupGreet Emma warmly by name in reception, acknowledge the accompanying parent, and escort her to Chair 6.
  4. Seating and inquirySeat her comfortably, provide eyewear, and ask: "Has anything felt loose, poking, or broken since your last visit?"
  5. Ergonomic setupAdjust the patient chair, your stool, and the light to achieve neutral seated alignment. Run the full 30-Second Reset.
  6. Appliance and tray walkthroughIdentify Emma's bracket components, archwire, and ligatures, then every instrument on the tray.
  7. Scripting and next stepsExplain the upcoming steps in clear, non-diagnostic language.
  8. Post-op simulationWalk the contaminated cassette to central sterilization receiving, trace the cleaning-to-autoclave path, and indicate where visit documentation occurs in Dolphin.
What is actually being assessed

Not speed, and not perfect recall. What your trainer is watching for is whether the sequence holds together, whether you stay oriented to the patient as a person while handling the technical steps, and whether you stop and ask when you hit something you are unsure of. Stopping to ask during the practical is a pass, not a fail.

Week 1 goal accomplished

"I know who this patient is. I know why they are here. I know where they sit. I recognize what is in their mouth. I recognize the instruments on my tray. I know how to communicate clearly and safely. And if I am ever unsure, I know to stop and ask."
