Clinical Protocol

Bite Pillows / Bite Blocks

Protecting newly bonded brackets by opening the bite, and never dismissing a patient who is still biting on a bracket.

Where this sits

Bite pillows finish a bonding appointment. Checking the bite is the last thing you do before the retractors come out, so build it into the routine rather than treating it as optional. This lesson is the companion to the Indirect Bonding Trays lesson.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  • Say why bite pillows are used and when they are needed.
  • Place a planned bite pillow in an indirect bonding case.
  • Place a bite pillow directly when it was not originally planned.
  • Inspect a finished bite pillow and recheck the bite before dismissing the patient.

Section 1What a bite pillow is for

Bite pillows protect our brackets

A bite pillow temporarily opens the patient's bite so the opposing teeth do not contact or bite directly onto a bracket. If a patient is biting on a newly bonded bracket, that bracket has a much greater chance of debonding.

Placing all of the brackets does not mean the bonding appointment is finished. The bite must always be checked after bonding.

Section 2Always check the bite after bonding

The most important rule

Always check the bite after bonding. Have the patient slowly bite together and look at the occlusion. Is an opposing tooth contacting a bracket? If no, bite pillows may not be necessary. If yes, the bite needs to be opened enough to protect that bracket. Do not dismiss the patient while they are actively biting on a bracket.

In an indirect bonding case, check for bite pillows before you remove the retractors. If the setup called for them, there will be a post-it note on the blackout container that held the trays. Check regardless of the note.

Section 3Bite pillows in an indirect bonding case

In many IDB cases the bite pillows were planned before the appointment, and the indirect bonding setup contains a pre-designed reservoir for the bite pillow. The reservoir determines the planned shape and location.

RESERVOIR PRESENT → FILL WITH BAND-LOK → SEAT → CURE → CHECK
Fill the reservoir completely

An incompletely filled reservoir creates voids, thin areas, irregular surfaces, food traps, and a weaker bite pillow. Fill it completely with Band-Lok before seating.

By default bite pillows go on the upper 6s unless the doctor indicates otherwise.

Section 4When no bite pillow was planned

Sometimes the setup does not include bite pillows. That does not mean the patient will never need them. Once all brackets are placed, have the patient bite down, examine the occlusion, and look specifically for bracket interference. If an opposing tooth is contacting a bracket, a bite pillow may need to be placed directly.

CHECK BITE → SAND CONDITION → ETCH → PRIME → PLACE BAND-LOK → CURE → RECHECK BITE

1. Identify the tooth

Determine where the bite pillow needs to go so the patient's bite opens enough to protect the brackets. The location may already be prescribed by the doctor.

2. Sand condition

Sand condition the occlusal surface where the bite pillow will be bonded. The surface needs to be clean and properly prepared for a reliable bond.

3. Etch

Etch the prepared enamel. Maintain good isolation and keep the etch off the soft tissue, then rinse thoroughly.

4. Dry and evaluate the enamel

Dry the tooth completely. The etched enamel should look white and frosty. If it does not, reassess the isolation and preparation before going on.

5. Prime

Apply the bonding primer to the prepared surface according to the bonding protocol.

6. Place the bite pillow

Place the Band-Lok and form the bite pillow into a smooth, solid shape. The goal is to open the bite enough to prevent bracket contact without creating unnecessary bulk.

7. Cure thoroughly

Light cure the bite pillow completely, making sure all of the material is cured.

Section 5Inspect, then recheck the bite

A bite pillow is not finished just because it is cured

Inspect it carefully. A finished bite pillow should have a smooth surface, solid material throughout, good bonding to the enamel, and no voids, holes, irregular ledges, or food traps. If you see a void or defect, correct it before dismissing the patient.

Recheck the occlusion

Never assume the bite pillow worked

Check the bite again. Confirm the bracket interference has actually been eliminated.

Section 6Planned versus unplanned

Planned in IDB

  1. Reservoir is already in the setup
  2. Fill the reservoir with Band-Lok
  3. Make sure it is completely filled
  4. Seat as planned
  5. Cure
  6. Inspect
  7. Check occlusion

Needed after the bite check

  1. No reservoir was planned
  2. Identify the appropriate tooth
  3. Sand condition the occlusal surface
  4. Etch and prime
  5. Place Band-Lok directly
  6. Cure
  7. Inspect and recheck occlusion

Section 7Avoid these mistakes

  • Forgetting to check the bite. The brackets may look perfect, but the patient could still be biting directly onto one.
  • Assuming no planned bite pillow means none is needed. The final occlusion decides whether additional bite opening is required.
  • Incompletely filling the IDB reservoir. This creates thin areas, voids, or food traps.
  • Poor isolation. Contamination compromises the bond.
  • Skipping surface prep on a direct placement. Direct bite pillows still require the full protocol: sand condition, etch, prime, place, cure.
  • Leaving voids or food traps. Inspect the bite pillow from multiple angles before dismissing the patient.
  • Failing to recheck the bite. Always verify that bracket interference has actually been eliminated.

Before dismissal

Bite pillow equals bracket protection

1. Check the bite. Is the patient biting on a bracket? 2. Protect the bracket. Use the planned IDB bite pillow or place one directly. 3. Bond it correctly. Sand condition, etch, prime, place, cure. 4. Inspect it. No voids, no food traps, smooth and solid. 5. Check the bite again. Make sure the opposing teeth are no longer contacting the brackets. The bonding appointment is not complete until the bite has been checked.

Lesson sign-off

Place a bite pillow under supervision, planned or direct. The bar is a smooth, solid pillow with no voids, and a rechecked bite where no tooth contacts a bracket.
