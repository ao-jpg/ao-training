Learning the Language of Orthodontics

Before you can work in the mouth, you need to be able to describe it.

The Golden Rule of Clinical Training

If you are unsure about any instruction, step, instrument, or material, stop and ask. We would rather you ask the same question five times than guess once while working with a patient.

By the end of today you will be able to

  • Use directional and anatomical terminology correctly when describing any tooth surface.
  • Explain the difference between overbite and overjet, and why confusing them matters.
  • Recognize open bite and crossbite on a typodont.
  • Describe the three Angle classifications of malocclusion.
  • Define bond, debond, reposition, and adjustment.

Orthodontics runs on precise language. Every direction, every surface, and every relationship has a specific word, and those words are how you and Dr. Lee will communicate at the chair. When he asks for something on the mesial of UR3, there is no room for interpretation. Today is about becoming fluent enough that instructions land without translation.

You are not expected to memorize all of this by five o'clock. You are expected to know where to find it and to start using the words out loud. Fluency comes from saying them, not from reading them.

Section 1Directional and anatomical terminology

These terms describe where something is. They work as a coordinate system for the mouth, and every one of them will come up in your first week at the chair.

The pair people mix up

Facial and buccal both mean the outer surface. The difference is only which teeth you are talking about: facial or labial for the front teeth near the lips, buccal for the back teeth near the cheeks. Lingual is always the tongue side, front or back, upper or lower.

Section 2Bite and malocclusion terminology

Occlusion
How the maxillary and mandibular teeth contact when the jaws close together.
Malocclusion
Any deviation from an ideal bite relationship or alignment. This is, broadly, what we treat.
Overbite (vertical)
The vertical overlap of the upper front teeth over the lower front teeth. Can be minimal, normal at roughly 1 to 2 mm, or a deep bite.
Overjet (horizontal)
The horizontal distance from the facial surface of the lower front teeth to the incisal edge of the upper front teeth. Around 2 mm is normal; 10 mm is significantly increased overjet.
Open bite
A vertical gap where opposing teeth do not touch when biting down.
Crossbite
When upper teeth bite inside the corresponding lower teeth. Can be anterior or posterior.

Overbite and overjet, side by side

A useful memory hook: overbite is how far the upper teeth come down over the lower. Overjet is how far they jut forward.

Section 3Angle classification of malocclusion

Angle classification describes the front-to-back relationship of the arches, and it is judged at the first molars. You will see it written on nearly every treatment card.

ClassAlso calledWhat it means
Class INeutroclusionNormal anteroposterior molar relationship. The mesiobuccal cusp of the upper first molar sits in the buccal groove of the lower first molar.
Class IIDistoclusionThe upper arch or teeth sit forward relative to the lower.
Class IIIMesioclusionThe lower arch or teeth sit forward relative to the upper.
Scope reminder

Recognizing a Class II molar relationship is useful vocabulary. Telling a patient or parent what class they are, or what it means for their treatment, is diagnosis, and diagnosis belongs to Dr. Lee. Learn the words so you can follow the conversation, not so you can lead it.

Section 4Common treatment actions

Bond
Attaching an orthodontic attachment (bracket, button, or tube) to enamel using adhesive.
Debond
Removing fixed appliances and cleaning residual adhesive from the enamel surfaces.
Reposition
Removing a bracket and rebonding it in a corrected position to improve tooth alignment.
Adjustment
A routine active treatment visit to check movement and change wires, ties, chains, or elastics.

These four words describe most of what happens in our clinic on any given day. By Day 3 you will be matching each one to its tray setup.
