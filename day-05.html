Dolphin Management & Infection Control

Knowing the patient before you meet them, and keeping the instruments moving one direction.

By the end of today you will be able to

  • Find a patient in Dolphin and open their Treatment Card.
  • Read a previous adjustment note and turn it into a setup.
  • Distinguish clean, contaminated, disinfected, and sterile.
  • Trace the five zones of central sterilization in the correct direction.
  • Demonstrate hand hygiene and correct donning and doffing of PPE.

Safe clinical practice rests on two systems that run underneath everything else: the electronic record that tells you what is supposed to happen, and the sterilization workflow that makes sure nothing travels between patients. Neither is glamorous. Both are where errors have consequences.

Section 1Dolphin and Treatment Cards

Before you see any patient, review their Dolphin record and confirm four things:

  1. Identity and photoVerify the patient name and match the photo. This is your check against seating the wrong person.
  2. Appointment detailsConfirm the scheduled procedure, the duration, and the assigned chair.
  3. Previous treatment noteReview what was placed, hygiene status, and any doctor instructions.
  4. Next-visit planCheck what archwires, power chain, or procedures are anticipated so you can pull materials before the patient sits down.

Read that note again with Day 1 and Day 2 in mind. PC 3-3 upper means power chain running from the upper right canine to the upper left canine. UL2 is the upper left lateral incisor. NiTi and SS are archwire materials. Everything on that card is vocabulary you already have.

This is the skill that makes you fast

A tech who reads the note and pulls the anticipated wire before the patient is seated saves the chair three or four minutes. Across a day of adjustments, that is the difference between running on time and running behind.

Section 2Aseptic classifications

Contaminated / dirty
Any instrument, item, or surface exposed to blood, saliva, or intraoral tissues.
Clean
An item with bioburden removed, but not yet sterilized. Clean does not mean sterile.
Disinfected
Clinical environmental surfaces treated with an EPA-registered hospital disinfectant wipe.
Sterile
Instruments processed through a validated heat sterilization cycle and sealed in intact packaging. If the packaging is compromised, the contents are no longer sterile regardless of what the indicator says.

Section 3Central sterilization: unidirectional flow

The rule

Instruments move in one continuous direction only. Never carry a contaminated item backward into a clean or sterile zone, not even for a second, not even to set it down while you get a door.

01

Receiving

Contaminated trays and used instruments arrive here. Nothing clean is ever set down in this zone.

↓
02

Cleaning

Ultrasonic bath or enzymatic wash. Bioburden comes off here.

↓
03

Packaging

Drying, inspection, pouching, and indicators. Inspect before you pouch; a pouched dirty instrument stays dirty.

↓
04

Sterilization

Autoclave or dry heat units. Do not overload; steam has to reach every surface.

↓
05

Sterile storage

Clean cassettes in covered storage, ready to be pulled for the next setup.

Key protocols

Hand hygiene
Wash with soap and water when hands are visibly soiled. Use alcohol rub before and after glove use.
Glove integrity
Single use only. Never wash or reuse gloves. Never touch a clean computer mouse, keyboard, or drawer handle with soiled gloves. This is the most frequently broken rule in any clinic, and it is the one that spreads contamination furthest.
Sharps safety
Discard all cut wire ends, scaling tips, and blades immediately into red sharps containers. Immediately means before you do anything else, not at the end of the appointment.
Special devices
Intraoral scanner tips (Alliedstar, iTero) and handpieces follow specialized manufacturer sterilization cycles. Never submerge handpieces in an ultrasonic bath.
