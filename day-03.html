Instruments & Common Appointments

Recognizing every instrument on sight, handling it safely, and knowing which tray it belongs on.

By the end of today you will be able to

  • Name all eight core instruments on sight, in any order.
  • Distinguish a ligature cutter from a distal-end cutter and explain why it matters.
  • Assemble a complete routine adjustment tray from sterile storage.
  • Describe the four core appointment types and what each requires.

Orthodontic instruments are precision-engineered, and several of them look similar enough at a glance to be confused. This week your focus is immediate visual recognition, safe handling, and matching instruments to appointment types. You are not expected to use them intraorally yet.

Safety rule for cutters

Never use a delicate ligature cutter to cut heavy archwire. It destroys the blades, and a damaged cutter is a hazard the next time someone picks it up.

Always use a distal-end cutter with an active safety-hold mechanism when cutting wire intraorally. The safety hold retains the cut fragment. Without it, a sharp wire end drops into the patient's mouth.

Section 3The four core appointment types

  1. Initial bonding — 60 to 90 minutesFull placement of brackets, buccal tubes, and the initial leveling and aligning archwires. Requires isolation, etchant, primer, adhesive, positioning gauges, curing lights, and wires. This is the longest and most setup-intensive appointment we run.
  2. Adjustment — 20 to 30 minutesThe routine active visit, and roughly eighty percent of daily clinic volume. Wire removal or exchange, O-tie replacement, power chain application, and progress evaluation. Most of what you do in your first months will be this.
  3. Single or additional bonding — about 30 minutesBonding a bracket to a newly erupted tooth, or rebonding an emergency debonded bracket.
  4. Reposition, or repo — about 30 minutesRemoving a bracket and bonding a new one in an adjusted position, to correct root or crown tip, torque, or height.
What to notice on the schedule

Because adjustments are the bulk of the day, the schedule moves fast and predictably. A single bond or repo dropped into that rhythm needs different materials pulled ahead of time. Getting used to reading the day's schedule and anticipating setups is a large part of becoming useful.
